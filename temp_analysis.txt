If your goal is **temperature analysis of PV modules**, not all diode parameters are equally informative. Some parameters are strongly temperature-dependent due to semiconductor physics, while others are more affected by manufacturing, aging, or fitting uncertainty.

## Most important parameters to analyze vs. module temperature

1. **(I_{ph}) (Photocurrent) — Very important**

   * Closely related to irradiance.
   * Increases slightly with temperature (typically around +0.03–0.06%/°C for silicon, depending on technology).
   * If irradiance changes, normalize or analyze irradiance and temperature together.

2. **(I_o) (Saturation current) — Most temperature-sensitive**

   * This is usually the key parameter for temperature studies.
   * It increases exponentially with temperature.
   * The increase in (I_o) is the primary reason that (V_{oc}) decreases as temperature rises.
   * It is much more meaningful to plot **(\log(I_o))** than (I_o), since (I_o) spans many orders of magnitude.

3. **(n) (Ideality factor) — Important**

   * Indicates the dominant recombination mechanism.
   * Often changes slightly with temperature.
   * Large variations can indicate changing recombination behavior or degradation.

4. **(R_s) (Series resistance) — Moderately important**

   * Can increase with temperature because metal and contact resistivity increase.
   * Also influenced by aging, solder joints, and interconnections.

5. **(R_{sh}) (Shunt resistance) — Less important for temperature**

   * Ideally remains high and nearly constant.
   * Variations are more commonly associated with defects, moisture ingress, PID, cracks, or leakage paths than temperature.

---

## Electrical parameters to compare with temperature

Besides diode parameters, the following are often more directly interpretable:

* **(V_{oc})** — Strong negative temperature coefficient (very important)
* **(P_{max})** — Decreases with temperature (very important)
* **Fill Factor (FF)** — Usually decreases with temperature
* **(V_{mpp})** — Decreases with temperature
* **(I_{sc})** — Slightly increases with temperature

These are the quantities manufacturers specify with temperature coefficients and are easier to validate against datasheets.

---

## Recommended analysis

If your objective is to understand how module temperature affects performance, I would prioritize plotting:

1. Module temperature vs. **(\log(I_o))**
2. Module temperature vs. **(V_{oc})**
3. Module temperature vs. **(P_{max})**
4. Module temperature vs. **(R_s)**
5. Module temperature vs. **Fill Factor**
6. Module temperature vs. **(I_{ph})** (while accounting for irradiance)

---

## Since you have weather data

Because your dataset already includes **module temperature and irradiance**, a better analysis is **2D rather than temperature alone**. For example:

* (\log(I_o)) vs. temperature (colored by irradiance)
* (R_s) vs. temperature (colored by irradiance)
* (P_{max}) vs. temperature (colored by irradiance)

This helps separate temperature effects from irradiance effects, since (I_{ph}), (I_{sc}), and power are strongly influenced by both.

### My recommendation

If you are writing a thesis, I'd focus on these six relationships:

* Module temperature → **(V_{oc})**
* Module temperature → **(\log(I_o))** (most physically meaningful diode parameter)
* Module temperature → **(R_s)**
* Module temperature → **Fill Factor**
* Module temperature → **Maximum power ((P_{max}))**
* Module temperature → **(I_{ph})** (with irradiance shown or normalized)

These provide a good balance between physically meaningful diode-model parameters and standard PV performance metrics.

% \subsection{Shunt Resistance (\(R_{sh}\))}
% For \(R_{sh}\) the NRMSE values indicate substantial prediction errors, reaching 0.322 for the silicon panel, while Pearson correlations remain weak (\(R=0.156\)–\(0.270\)) and the \(R^2\) values are close to zero.

The weaker Pearson correlations and \(R^2\) values suggest that the predicted \(R_{sh}\) values do not consistently reproduce the variation observed in the reference parameters. A possible reason is the limited sensitivity of the J-V curve to changes in $R_{sh}$, particularly when the shunt resistance is relatively high. In the single-diode model, the shunt term appears as $(V + J R_s)/R_{sh}$, and when $R_{sh}$ is large this contribution becomes very small, causing $\partial J / \partial R_{sh}$ to approach zero. As a result, even substantial variations in $R_{sh}$ produce only minimal changes in the measured J-V curve, leaving the model with little voltage-dependent information from which to infer the true sample-to-sample variation. In addition, the extraction of single-diode parameters can be non-unique, meaning that similar J-V characteristics may be obtained from different combinations of \(R_{sh}\) and other diode parameters \cite{rhouma_simple_2017,humada_solar_2016}. Consequently, variations in \(R_{sh}\) extracted from the J-V curves may not correspond to a unique or easily learnable relationship, making detailed prediction of this parameter more challenging.

% \subsection{Summary}
% These results show that parameters directly associated with prominent and identifiable features of the J-V curve, such as $V_{oc}$ and $J_{sc}$ are generally predicted more reliably because variations in these parameters produce observable changes in specific regions or characteristics of the curve. In contrast, parameters that depend on the precise shape and position of the knee region, such as $P_{mpp}$ and FF, exhibit greater sensitivity to local prediction errors, since small deviations in the voltage-current relationship around this nonlinear transition can propagate into larger errors in the extracted quantities. The diode parameters $R_{sh}$ show weaker correlation and numerical errors, which is consistent with the inherent coupling and non-uniqueness involved in extracting single-diode parameters from J-V characteristics. This is challenging because multiple diode parameters can simultaneously influence the shape of the J-V curve, allowing different parameter combinations to produce similar electrical characteristics. Previous studies have similarly highlighted that different combinations of single-diode parameters can produce nearly identical J-V curves, making individual parameter estimation more challenging \cite{sabadus_nature_2021, laudani_hybrid_2015, humada_solar_2016}.
